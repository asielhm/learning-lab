Prepare your solutions ·

Concentration, aliquot volume and acid mass are linked: m = c × V × M, using V in litres and the molar mass M = 60.052 g/mol. Changing a preparation value starts a new trial; previous notebook rows remain. Mass means dissolved ethanoic acid, not the total mass of vinegar. Choose a calibrated pipette capacity for the aliquot. The acid-minus-base reading is a stoichiometric balance; a negative value means excess base, not a negative number of particles.

Why does the colour change?

Ethanoic acid in vinegar reacts with hydroxide ions from NaOH. The reaction is CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O, with a 1:1 mole ratio. Phenolphthalein is colourless below about pH 8.2 and pink in alkaline solution. A local pink streak can appear where a drop enters, then disappear while acid remains. Swirling mixes an addition through the whole sample; judge the colour of the mixed solution.

This is a weak acid–strong base titration: its equivalence point is alkaline, not pH 7. Near equivalence, one small drop can cause a large pH rise. Close the tap early and finish drop by drop.

Check your reasoning: why switch to single drops?

The pH changes steeply near equivalence. A large addition can overshoot the first persistent faint pink. Deep pink means excess alkali; start a fresh trial.

From observation to concentration.

c(acid) = c(base) × V(base) ÷ V(acid). Here c means concentration in mol/L; V means volume. Use the same volume units in the ratio. Use the concentrations and volumes selected in the preparation controls. An aliquot is a measured portion of a solution.

For example, a 22.50 mL titre gives 0.100 × 22.50 ÷ 25.0 = 0.0900 mol/L in the diluted vinegar. Multiply by the known dilution factor to calculate the original vinegar concentration.

Ideal equilibrium model: monoprotic ethanoic acid, Kₐ = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, 25°C and additive volumes. It omits other acids, measurement uncertainty and mixing gradients. The pipette begins with the selected measured aliquot; filling, conditioning glassware and removing air bubbles are assumed.
