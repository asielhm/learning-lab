    Connect the model to the physics

    Distance, displacement and motion

    Distance is path length; displacement is change in position with direction. Speed is scalar; velocity has direction. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. The trolley’s direction can change while position remains positive.

    Worked example. Move 10 m east then 4 m west: distance = 14 m, displacement = 6 m east.

    Graphs and constant acceleration

    The gradient of a position–time graph is velocity; the gradient of velocity–time is acceleration. Signed area under velocity–time gives displacement. For constant a: v = u + at and s = ut + ½at².

    Worked example. At u = 4 m/s, a = −2 m/s² and t = 4 s, displacement is 0 m, although total distance is 8 m.

    Free fall

    Without air resistance, all objects at the same location share acceleration g ≈ 9.81 m/s², regardless of mass. The vacuum model releases objects from rest.

    Worked example. From 20 m, t = √(2h/g) ≈ 2.02 s and impact speed ≈ 19.8 m/s. Feather and ball land together in a vacuum.

    Projectile components

    In the ideal model, horizontal velocity remains constant while vertical acceleration is −g. Resolve u into u cosθ and u sinθ. At the highest point, vertical velocity is zero but horizontal velocity usually is not.

    Worked example. At 20 m/s and 45° from level ground, flight time ≈ 2.88 s and range ≈ 40.8 m. Complementary angles give equal range only for equal launch/landing heights without drag.

    Reaction and braking

    Thinking distance = speed × reaction time. With constant braking deceleration, braking distance = v²/(2a). Total stopping distance adds both. This idealised model does not simulate tyre or road mechanics.

    Worked example. At 20 m/s, 0.7 s reaction time and 5 m/s² braking deceleration, thinking distance is 14 m and braking distance 40 m: 54 m total.

    Catch-up problems and delayed starts

    Use one shared clock and one coordinate origin. Before its release, the delayed object remains at its starting position; afterwards use x = x₀ + v(t − delay). Meeting requires equal positions at the same time, not equal distances travelled.

    Worked example. A begins at 20 m at 3 m/s. B begins at 0 m after 2 s, at 5 m/s. Solve 20 + 3t = 5(t − 2): t = 15 s, x = 65 m. B has travelled for 13 s. If the gap grows after B starts, there is no future catch.
