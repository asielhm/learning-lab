Electricity and electrostatics
Preparation and worked examples

Charge is a property of matter, and current is the rate at which charge passes a point. Voltage is energy transferred per unit charge between points, not the amount of current stored in a wire. Resistance describes the relationship between voltage and current for a component under defined conditions.

A circuit needs an appropriate closed path for sustained current. Series components share current; parallel branches share voltage between the same two nodes. Use V = IR for an ohmic resistor at constant temperature. Coulomb’s law describes electrostatic force under a different model, and electrical safety requires distinguishing excess current from an imbalance caused by leakage.

A 2 kW heater used for 3 hours transfers 6 kWh of energy. A bill charges for energy, not simply the number of appliances or the supply voltage. A fuse and an RCD respond to different faults, which is why one protective device does not automatically replace another.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Q is electric charge in coulombs (C); I current in amperes (A); t time in seconds (s): I = Q/t. V is potential difference in volts (V), energy transferred per charge: V = E/Q, where E is energy in joules (J). R is resistance in ohms (Ω): V = IR for an ohmic component at constant conditions. Current flows; energy is transferred, not current used up.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

In series, components share one path and current; Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … adds resistances. In parallel, branches share potential difference and currents add; 1/Rtotal = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … . Subscripts label components. A node is a junction; a closed loop is a complete conducting route. Always distinguish resistance from the physical resistor component.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

F = k|q₁q₂|/r² gives electrostatic force magnitude F (N) between point charges q₁,q₂ (C) separated by r (m). k ≈ 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² in vacuum. | | means absolute value; r² is r × r. Like charges repel and unlike attract. Doubling separation quarters force if charges are unchanged.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

P = VI is electrical power P in watts (W); V voltage (V) and I current (A). E = Pt is energy E (J) transferred in t seconds at constant power. 1 W = 1 J/s. For bills, kWh means kilowatt-hours: power in kW times time in hours, an energy unit; 1 kWh = 3.6 million J.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A fuse melts on excessive current; a circuit breaker opens an overcurrent circuit; a residual-current device compares outgoing and returning currents to detect leakage. Earthing provides a protective route for fault current. Isolation means disconnecting and verifying the supply is off. The activity is a model of safety decisions, not a qualification to work on live mains equipment.

03

Circuits and power

Understand the ideas

  • There is one conducting path, so the same current passes through each series component.
  • Each branch is connected between the same two circuit nodes.
  • Another branch adds a conducting path; two equal parallel resistors have half the resistance of one.
  • Voltage is energy per charge and current is charge per time, so VI is energy per time.
  • Both see the same V, so I = V/R is larger for the smaller resistance.
  • P = VI = 6 × 0.4. Result: 2.4 W.
  • P = VI = 12 × 2. Result: 24 W.
  • P = VI = 9 × 0.5. Result: 4.5 W.
  • P = VI = 230 × 8. Result: 1.84e+03 W.
  • P = VI = 5 × 0.2. Result: 1 W.

Coulomb’s law

Understand the ideas

  • Like-sign charges repel; unlike-sign charges attract.
  • The point-charge formula uses the separation of the charge locations in SI units.
  • F is proportional to 1/r².
  • The product of signs determines attraction or repulsion; absolute magnitudes determine force size.
  • Field direction is the force direction on a small positive test charge; this is an extension idea.
