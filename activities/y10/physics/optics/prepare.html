Optics and cameras
Preparation and worked examples

A ray represents a direction in which light travels in the geometrical model. A normal is a line perpendicular to a surface, and reflection and refraction angles are measured from it. An image can be real, where rays converge, or virtual, where their backward extensions appear to meet.

Refraction occurs when propagation speed changes across a boundary; frequency stays the same at a stationary boundary. Total internal reflection requires higher-to-lower refractive index and a sufficiently large incidence angle. Thin-lens equations relate object distance, image distance and focal length under approximations. Decide image type and sign convention before interpreting a calculator answer.

A camera needs a focused real image on its sensor, while a magnifying glass commonly gives a virtual image to the eye. With a converging lens of focal length 10 cm and object distance 30 cm, a screen at 15 cm captures a real inverted image half the object’s height.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

The normal is an imaginary line perpendicular to a surface. θi = θr means angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, both measured from the normal, not the surface. A virtual image is located where rays appear to originate; a real image is where rays actually converge. A mirror allows visibility only if a reflected light path reaches the observer’s eye.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂ is Snell’s law. n is refractive index, n = c/v, a ratio without units; c is vacuum light speed and v speed in the medium. θ₁,θ₂ are angles from the normal; subscripts distinguish media. sin is the sine function; use inverse sine to recover an angle, with the calculator in the intended angle unit.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Total internal reflection requires travel from higher refractive index n₁ to lower n₂. The critical angle θc obeys sinθc = n₂/n₁; beyond it no transmitted ray exists in the simple ray model. “Critical” marks the limiting refracted angle of 90°. An optical fibre guides light using this effect, with losses and material limitations.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

1/f = 1/do + 1/di uses focal length f, object distance do and image distance di in the same unit. Here a converging lens has f > 0; a real object do > 0; real image di > 0 and virtual di < 0. Magnification m = −di/do = hi/ho, where h denotes height. Negative m means inverted; |m| gives size ratio. do = f is a limiting case with image at infinity.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A camera forms a real image on a sensor. Focusing adjusts lens-to-sensor distance for the subject distance. Aperture is the opening admitting light; focal length describes the lens’s focusing strength and affects angle of view. A ray diagram follows selected rays from one object point; it does not imply that only those rays exist.

Understand the ideas

  • The refracted angle can reach 90° only for the higher-to-lower direction.

  • Refraction angle r = 90° at the critical angle.

  • TIR requires the opposite index direction as well as sufficiently large incidence.

Ray Detective · concepts and examples

Pair each clue with the correct rule.

Measure angles from the normal, not the surface. Total internal reflection needs travel from higher to lower refractive index.

Compare one model solution

Plane mirror: incidence 35° → Reflection 35° from the normal; Air to glass, oblique incidence → Bends towards the normal; Glass to air above critical angle → Total internal reflection; Ray through the centre of a thin lens → Continues approximately straight

Classify the image.

Real images form where rays actually converge and can be caught on a screen. Virtual images are located by extending diverging rays backwards.

Compare one model solution

Converging lens, object beyond 2f → Real; Converging lens, object between lens and f → Virtual; Plane mirror → Virtual; Diverging lens, real object → Virtual; Converging lens, object at 2f → Real; Camera image on a sensor → Real

What changes at an air-to-glass boundary?

Frequency is fixed by the source across a stationary boundary. Speed and wavelength change together, v = fλ.

Compare one model solution

Light frequency → Stays the same; Light speed → Decreases; Light wavelength → Becomes shorter; Direction at normal incidence → Remains straight
