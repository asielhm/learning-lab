A wave is a travelling disturbance. In a mechanical wave, particles can oscillate around their positions while the disturbance transfers energy. Amplitude measures maximum displacement, frequency counts oscillations per second and wavelength measures spacing between equivalent neighbouring points.

Wave speed relates frequency and wavelength through v = fλ. Sound is a longitudinal mechanical disturbance and needs a medium. Electromagnetic radiation can travel through a vacuum. Reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference are different behaviours; use the boundary conditions and the medium to decide which explanation fits an observation.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

An echo uses a reflected sound pulse, and its travel time covers the outward and return journey. A 0.20 s echo at 340 m/s corresponds to a reflector 34 m away, not 68 m. A passing siren also illustrates Doppler shift: received frequency can change without the source changing its own emission frequency.

01 · Wave speed, frequency and wavelength

A wave transfers energy without a net transport of the medium. v = fλ and T = 1/f. In a transverse wave, oscillations are perpendicular to travel; in a longitudinal wave they are parallel.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

v = fλ: v is wave speed (m/s), f frequency (Hz, cycles per second), λ wavelength (m). T = 1/f is period, seconds per cycle. Amplitude is maximum displacement from equilibrium, the undisturbed position. A transverse oscillation is perpendicular to wave travel; a longitudinal one is parallel. Particles oscillate locally while the disturbance carries energy.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. A 5 Hz wave with wavelength 2 m travels at 10 m/s. Watch one marked particle rather than following a travelling crest.
02 · Pitch, loudness and echoes

Higher frequency means higher pitch. Larger pressure amplitude generally means a louder sound at the same frequency; it does not raise the frequency. Sound needs a medium. Echo distance is half the sound’s round-trip travel.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Pitch is perception mainly related to frequency; loudness depends on intensity and hearing response. An echo is reflected sound. d = vt/2 estimates reflector distance d (m), with sound speed v (m/s) and round-trip delay t (s); divide by two because sound travels out and back. Sound needs a medium, the material carrying the disturbance.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. At 340 m/s, an echo after 0.50 s comes from a surface 85 m away.
03 · The Doppler effect

A moving source crowds wavefronts ahead and spreads them behind. For a stationary observer and approaching subsonic source, f′ = fv/(v − vₛ); for recession use v + vₛ.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Doppler effect is a change in received frequency due to relative motion. For a source approaching a stationary listener, fobs = femit v/(v − vs): observed and emitted frequencies are in Hz, v is sound speed and vs source speed through the medium, in the same units. It requires vs < v; the source’s emitted frequency need not change.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For a 600 Hz siren moving at 20 m/s through air at 340 m/s, a stationary observer hears about 638 Hz ahead and 567 Hz behind.
04 · Electromagnetic radiation

Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma radiation are all transverse electromagnetic waves. All travel at c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum; frequency rises as wavelength falls.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Electromagnetic waves are linked electric and magnetic disturbances that can travel through vacuum. In vacuum c = fλ, with c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s, f in Hz and λ in m. The spectrum orders waves by wavelength or frequency. “Ionising” means able to remove bound electrons; risk also depends on exposure and energy absorbed.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Microwaves can carry communications; infrared is used in thermal imaging; X-rays form images but require controlled exposure. The spectrum diagram is arranged by band, not a linear wavelength scale.
05 · Wave behaviour at boundaries

Reflection changes direction within the original medium. Refraction changes speed and usually direction at a boundary, while frequency stays the same. Diffraction spreads waves at gaps; spreading is more pronounced when the gap is comparable to wavelength.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Reflection sends a wave back; refraction changes direction when its speed changes across a boundary; diffraction spreads a wave at gaps or edges. Frequency stays unchanged at a stationary boundary, while speed and wavelength can change. Superposition adds displacements where waves meet; interference describes resulting reinforcement or cancellation.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Water waves entering shallower water usually slow down: unchanged f and smaller v mean a smaller wavelength.
