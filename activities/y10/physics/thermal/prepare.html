Thermal physics
Preparation and worked examples

Temperature describes thermal state; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Internal energy is not the same as temperature or heat. Two objects at the same temperature can contain very different amounts of internal energy because their masses and substances differ.

For a temperature change without a phase transition, use Q = mcΔT with an appropriate specific heat capacity. During a transition at fixed pressure, latent heat can change state without raising the temperature of a pure substance. Conduction, convection and radiation provide different transfer mechanisms and can act together in the same situation.

Heating 0.50 kg of water by 20 °C requires about 42 kJ using c = 4200 J/(kg °C), ignoring the container and losses. A kettle supplies more energy if some heats its casing or escapes to the surroundings. Insulation changes transfer rates, not the definition of temperature.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Temperature relates to average particle kinetic energy; internal energy includes microscopic kinetic and interaction energies. Thermal equilibrium means no net heat flow between bodies in contact. T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15: T is kelvin temperature, t Celsius reading. A temperature difference of 1 K equals one of 1 °C, but absolute zeros differ.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Conduction transfers energy through interactions without bulk material flow. Convection transfers energy with moving fluid. Radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves and needs no material medium. A temperature gradient is a spatial temperature difference. A good insulator reduces transfer rate; it does not create heat or stop transfer forever.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Q = mcΔT: Q is energy transferred by heating (J), m mass (kg), c specific heat capacity (J/(kg·K)) and ΔT = final − initial temperature (K or °C difference). Specific heat capacity is energy needed per kilogram per degree change. This expression applies within one phase when c is approximately constant and energy losses are accounted for.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Q = mL: Q is energy transferred (J), m mass changing phase (kg), L specific latent heat (J/kg). Phase means solid, liquid or gas. During an ideal change of phase at fixed pressure, energy changes particle arrangement without changing temperature. Melting and boiling have different L values; a heating graph’s flat section is not zero energy input.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

In a thermally isolated system, heat lost by hotter parts equals heat gained by cooler parts. ΣQ = 0 means add all signed heat transfers; Q = mc(Tf − Ti) for each part. Tf is common final temperature, Ti that part’s initial temperature, m its mass and c its specific heat capacity. Include a container’s heat capacity or justify neglecting it.

03

Specific heat capacity

Understand the ideas

  • Specific heat capacity is energy per unit mass per temperature rise.
  • Use the temperature change, not the final temperature alone.
  • J/(kg·K) is also valid because a temperature interval has the same numerical size.
  • ΔT = E/(mc).
  • E_supplied exceeds energy gained by the sample; E_supplied/(mΔT) is too large if losses are ignored.
  • E = mcΔT = 0.2 × 4200 × 5. Result: 4.2e+03 J.
  • E = mcΔT = 0.5 × 4200 × 10. Result: 2.1e+04 J.
  • E = mcΔT = 0.3 × 4200 × 20. Result: 2.52e+04 J.
  • E = mcΔT = 1 × 4200 × 15. Result: 6.3e+04 J.
  • E = mcΔT = 0.4 × 4200 × 8. Result: 1.34e+04 J.

05

Thermal equilibrium

Understand the ideas

  • Thermal equilibrium means no net thermal transfer between them.
  • Energy conservation relates the two changes, neglecting the container and losses.
  • Equal heat capacities and masses give the arithmetic mean.
  • The larger heat capacity weighs the final temperature towards its initial temperature.
  • The simple formula assumes the same c, no phase change and an isolated system with negligible container capacity.
  • For the same material, Tf = (m₁T₁ + m₂T₂)/(m₁ + m₂). Result: 44 °C.
  • For the same material, Tf = (m₁T₁ + m₂T₂)/(m₁ + m₂). Result: 50 °C.
  • For the same material, Tf = (m₁T₁ + m₂T₂)/(m₁ + m₂). Result: 30 °C.

Which mechanism is described?

Conduction transfers energy through interactions in matter; convection moves fluid; radiation needs no material medium.

Compare one model solution

Energy along a metal spoon → Conduction; Warm water rising in a pan → Convection; Sunlight crossing empty space → Radiation; Air circulating above a heater → Convection; Infrared from a glowing element to your skin → Radiation; Heat through a solid wall → Conduction

Match the energy equation or condition.

Heat is energy in transfer because of a temperature difference. Equal temperature does not imply equal internal energy.

Compare one model solution

Temperature changes without change of state → Q = mcΔT; Melting at the melting point → Q = mL; Thermal equilibrium → Equal temperature; no net heat flow; Ideal insulated mixing → Energy lost = energy gained

Order the stages of heating ice into steam at atmospheric pressure.

During a pure-substance phase change at fixed pressure, energy changes the state while temperature stays constant.

Compare one model solution

Warm ice below 0 °C → 1 · first; Melt ice at 0 °C → 2; Warm liquid water → 3; Reach 100 °C → 4; Boil at 100 °C → 5; Warm steam above 100 °C → 6

Energy Budget Kitchen · concepts and examples

Warm 0.50 kg of water from 20 °C to 40 °C. Use an insulated vessel.

Q = 0.50 × 4200 × 20 = 42 000 J = 42 kJ. Vessel heat capacity is ignored.

Compare one model solution

Heater energy / kJ: 42; Vessel: Insulated: 100% retained

Warm 0.25 kg of water from 20 °C to 60 °C. Keep all heater energy in the water.

0.25 × 4200 × 40 = 42 kJ. In the leaky model, only 80% of supplied energy reaches the water.

Compare one model solution

Heater energy / kJ: 42; Vessel: Insulated: 100% retained

Warm 0.10 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C, with ideal insulation.

21 kJ raises this sample by 50 °C. This model stays below boiling and ignores the heat capacity of the apparatus.

Compare one model solution

Heater energy / kJ: 21; Vessel: Insulated: 100% retained
