Temperature describes thermal state; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Internal energy is not the same as temperature or heat. Two objects at the same temperature can contain very different amounts of internal energy because their masses and substances differ.

For a temperature change without a phase transition, use Q = mcΔT with an appropriate specific heat capacity. During a transition at fixed pressure, latent heat can change state without raising the temperature of a pure substance. Conduction, convection and radiation provide different transfer mechanisms and can act together in the same situation.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

Heating 0.50 kg of water by 20 °C requires about 42 kJ using c = 4200 J/(kg °C), ignoring the container and losses. A kettle supplies more energy if some heats its casing or escapes to the surroundings. Insulation changes transfer rates, not the definition of temperature.

01 · Temperature and internal energy

Temperature describes thermal state and relates to average particle kinetic energy. Internal energy includes microscopic kinetic and potential energy. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Temperature relates to average particle kinetic energy; internal energy includes microscopic kinetic and interaction energies. Thermal equilibrium means no net heat flow between bodies in contact. T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15: T is kelvin temperature, t Celsius reading. A temperature difference of 1 K equals one of 1 °C, but absolute zeros differ.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. A large warm bath may contain more internal energy than a tiny hotter cup, even though the cup has a higher temperature.
02 · Three transfer mechanisms

Conduction transfers energy through interactions and mobile electrons in metals. Convection is bulk fluid motion caused by density differences in a gravitational field. Thermal radiation is electromagnetic and can pass through a vacuum.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Conduction transfers energy through interactions without bulk material flow. Convection transfers energy with moving fluid. Radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves and needs no material medium. A temperature gradient is a spatial temperature difference. A good insulator reduces transfer rate; it does not create heat or stop transfer forever.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Touch a metal spoon, watch heated water circulate, and compare matt black and shiny surfaces. Visible particle/ray speeds in the model are illustrative.
03 · Specific heat capacity

E = mcΔT, with E in J, mass in kg and c in J/(kg·°C) or J/(kg·K). A temperature difference has the same numerical value in kelvin and °C. Ideal calculations neglect losses and container heat capacity.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Q = mcΔT: Q is energy transferred by heating (J), m mass (kg), c specific heat capacity (J/(kg·K)) and ΔT = final − initial temperature (K or °C difference). Specific heat capacity is energy needed per kilogram per degree change. This expression applies within one phase when c is approximately constant and energy losses are accounted for.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Heating 0.50 kg of water by 10°C takes 0.50 × 4200 × 10 = 21,000 J.
04 · Changes of state

During a change of state at fixed pressure, supplied energy changes phase rather than temperature. E = mL. Our ice/water curve uses 0°C melting, cᵢ = 2100, c𝑤 = 4200 J/(kg·°C) and Lf = 334,000 J/kg; heating ends below boiling.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Q = mL: Q is energy transferred (J), m mass changing phase (kg), L specific latent heat (J/kg). Phase means solid, liquid or gas. During an ideal change of phase at fixed pressure, energy changes particle arrangement without changing temperature. Melting and boiling have different L values; a heating graph’s flat section is not zero energy input.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Melting 0.20 kg of ice at 0°C needs 66,800 J. First warming ice from −20°C requires additional energy.
05 · Thermal equilibrium

In an isolated mixture, energy lost by the warmer sample equals energy gained by the cooler sample. Equal final temperature does not mean equal internal energies. No phase change is modelled in the mixing investigation.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

In a thermally isolated system, heat lost by hotter parts equals heat gained by cooler parts. ΣQ = 0 means add all signed heat transfers; Q = mc(Tf − Ti) for each part. Tf is common final temperature, Ti that part’s initial temperature, m its mass and c its specific heat capacity. Include a container’s heat capacity or justify neglecting it.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Mix 0.20 kg of water at 80°C with 0.30 kg at 20°C: Tf = (0.2×80 + 0.3×20)/0.5 = 44°C.
