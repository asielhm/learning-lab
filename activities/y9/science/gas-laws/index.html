Why Kelvin, not Celsius?

Gas-law ratios use absolute temperature: T(K) = temperature(°C) + 273.15. For example, 300 K to 600 K is a doubling; 20°C to 40°C is not. The graph illustrates an ideal gas. Real gases can depart from these laws, particularly near condensation.

CONNECT THE THREE EXPERIMENTS

Same gas. Different constraints.

Boyle–Mariotte

Constant temperature

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
Charles

Constant pressure

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂
Gay-Lussac / Amontons

Constant volume

P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂

For a fixed amount of ideal gas, all three follow from PV/T = constant. Always use Kelvin for temperature.
