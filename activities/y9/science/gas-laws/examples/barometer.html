Y9 SCIENCE · ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE IN ACTION

A mercury barometer

Air has weight and its particles exert pressure. How can that pressure support a tall column of liquid metal? Change the atmospheric pressure and watch the mercury find a new balance.

Patm ≈ ρgh

Patm · atmospheric pressure
Pressure of the air on the reservoir. Pa means pascal: 1 Pa = 1 N/m². A newton (N) is a force unit; m² means square metre of area.
ρ (rho) · mercury density
Mass per volume: 13,595.1 kg/m³ at the reference temperature 0°C. kg/m³ means kilograms per cubic metre.
g · gravitational acceleration
9.80665 m/s² in this model. Gravity gives the mercury its weight.
h · vertical height difference
Measure between the reservoir surface and the mercury surface inside the tube. Use metres in the equation: 760 mm = 0.760 m.

04 / CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Explain what you can see.

Why is there mercury above the bowl?

The glass tube is sealed at its top and open below the reservoir surface. The space above the column contains almost no air, so its pressure is very small. The atmosphere presses on the exposed reservoir, and the connected liquid transmits pressure to the tube’s submerged end.

Equilibrium means the liquid has settled with no net motion. The pressure difference then supports the weight of the raised liquid. Increasing atmospheric pressure supports a taller column; reducing it allows mercury to return to the reservoir. The total mercury amount stays fixed, so the reservoir level also moves slightly.

Remember: stronger air pressure supports a taller mercury column.

The low-pressure space above is often called a Torricellian vacuum. There is a little mercury vapour in a real instrument; this model neglects its very small pressure. The vacuum is not an upward pulling force.

Read the numbers and units carefully.

Pressure is force per area. The atmosphere has an absolute pressure of about 101,325 Pa at the standard reference, not zero. A tyre gauge may show zero when its pressure equals the outside atmosphere because it measures the difference.

kPa means kilopascal: 1 kPa = 1,000 Pa. atm is an atmosphere, a pressure unit defined as exactly 101,325 Pa. mmHg is a pressure unit based on a reference mercury column. The column’s height h is a length measured in millimetres; at the chosen reference density and gravity its numerical height matches the pressure expressed in mmHg.

Use the vertical difference: do not measure from the bottom of the reservoir or include the empty space above the column. A taller glass tube does not by itself make the atmospheric pressure higher.

Everyday connection: atmospheric pressure is generally lower high on a mountain because less air lies above. Weather-related pressure changes can also move a barometer, but one reading alone cannot predict the weather.

What is kept fixed in this model?

Mercury density is held at its 0°C reference value and gravity at the standard value. Above-column pressure, surface-tension effects (the surface interactions that curve the liquid boundary against glass) and real fluid oscillations are neglected. The reservoir area is 20 times the tube’s inner area in the volume model, so its surface shifts slightly as the column changes. The virtual height overlay follows this surface; vessel widths in the drawing are illustrative. Atmospheric pressure ranges from 50 to 110 kPa, covering broad altitude and pressure comparisons rather than ordinary weather at one location. Smooth motion is illustrative, not a prediction of settling time.

Mercury is toxic. This is a virtual instrument; do not attempt to construct or handle a mercury barometer.
