Y9 SCIENCE · THE BLOOD TRANSPORT GAME

Transport Dispatch

Deliver what a tissue needs, or take away what it needs to remove. Choose for the named cargo: several components can help at the same location.

How to play

  1. Read the cargo and starting point. Click a component card, or drag it into the yellow carrier bay.
  2. Click the destination organ on the map and launch. Follow the highlighted route.
  3. Answer the explanation question. Retry wrong choices; hints and revealed solutions help you learn.

Three stars for an unaided first-time delivery, two after a wrong attempt, one with a hint and zero for a revealed mission. No countdown; these game scores stay on this page and are not teacher test records.

Choose the carrier

Read the route, not the drawing’s geography.

From a body tissue, blood returns to the right heart, goes to the lungs and then reaches the left heart before another systemic delivery. Systemic means the body circulation outside the lungs. The left-heart starting point means the carrier is already in circulating blood; cells are not being made there. Simplified branch lines may cross on the drawing without forming anatomical junctions.

Oxygen unloads from haemoglobin; glucose can leave plasma through exchange and membrane-transport routes; carbon dioxide is carried mainly as bicarbonate. The map token represents a delivery travelling within blood, not a whole red cell entering an organ’s tissue. Blood colour is always red, even where return routes are drawn purple.

Biology sources, game inspiration and model limits

Original Learning Lab explanations, illustrations and game. Biology checked against OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, chapters 18 and 20, using its public textbook source. The further-reading links are optional; every instruction needed to learn and play is on this platform.

  • OpenStax: an overview of blood — composition, transport, regulation and protection.
  • OpenStax: erythrocytes — haemoglobin and oxygen carriage.
  • OpenStax: leukocytes and platelets and haemostasis — immune functions, plug and fibrin.
  • OpenStax: vessel structure and function and capillary exchange.
  • PhET public membrane-transport model description — inspiration for visible particles and qualitative gradient investigation.
  • Blood Typing Game: public educational-game description — inspiration for choosing a blood-related action and learning from its consequence. Our game teaches component transport, rather than transfusion compatibility.

Cells and molecules are enlarged, cell counts are not proportional, and motion is slowed for visibility. Wall thickness, vessel size and capillary speed are qualitative comparisons. The transport map shows route order rather than anatomy; it does not predict clinical delivery times. The centrifuge is a pre-balanced, closed teaching model, not a blood-handling procedure.
