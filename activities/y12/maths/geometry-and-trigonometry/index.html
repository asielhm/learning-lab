Geometry connects lengths, angles and position. A trigonometric ratio compares side lengths in a right-angled triangle for a specified angle. An inverse trigonometric function retrieves an angle from a ratio, but its principal answer may not describe every possible angle in a larger problem.

Choose a diagram and label known quantities before selecting a formula. Degree and radian measures describe the same turn using different units: 180° = π radians. A calculator mode must match the inputs. Scale drawings are evidence for a plan, not a substitute for exact relationships or the required conditions of a theorem.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

To estimate the height of a tower on level ground, a measured elevation angle and horizontal distance can define a right triangle. If the observer’s eye is above ground, add that height to the calculated rise. A correct tangent calculation can still give an incomplete answer if the model omits the observer.

01 · Choose the right triangle and ratio

For a specified angle in a right-angled triangle, sine compares opposite to hypotenuse, cosine adjacent to hypotenuse and tangent opposite to adjacent. The opposite and adjacent labels depend on the chosen angle. The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. With horizontal distance 20 m and elevation angle 30°, rise = 20 tan(30°) ≈ 11.55 m.
  2. If eye height is 1.60 m, the model tower height is about 13.15 m.
  3. The assumptions include level ground and an angle measured from the horizontal.
02 · Use degrees and radians consistently

A radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius. A full circle measures 2π radians, equal to 360°. The formulas s = rθ and sector area = ½r²θ require θ in radians.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. 60° = π/3 radians.
  2. At radius 6 cm, arc length for 60° is 6 × π/3 = 2π cm.
  3. Entering 60 into s = rθ without converting would not describe the same angle.
03 · Check conditions before choosing a formula

Pythagoras applies to right-angled triangles; sine and cosine rules extend to more general triangles. Draw and label the known data, identify possible ambiguous cases and test whether the answer fits the diagram and triangle constraints.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For sides a = 5, b = 7 and included angle C = 60°, c² = 5² + 7² − 2(5)(7)cos60° = 39.
  2. Therefore c = √39 ≈ 6.245.
  3. The side length is positive and lies between |7 − 5| and 7 + 5.
