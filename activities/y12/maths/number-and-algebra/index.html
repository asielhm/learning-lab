Algebra expresses relationships between quantities so they can be manipulated and solved. Powers describe repeated factors, logarithms undo exponentiation, and sequences describe ordered patterns. A symbol is meaningful only after its quantity, domain and any units are defined.

An equation asks for values that make two sides equal. A numerical solver can find a candidate in a region, but it does not automatically prove that all solutions have been found or that each is valid in the original domain. Financial and sequence models also require careful interpretation of time periods and rates.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

With annual growth of 5%, 1000 becomes 1000 × 1.05ⁿ after n years in a simple compound-growth model. A logarithm can estimate when a target is reached, but the required whole year may need rounding upward. Arithmetic growth by a fixed addition is a different model.

01 · Powers and logarithms undo each other

If bˣ = a with b > 0 and b ≠ 1, then log_b(a) = x. A logarithm asks for an exponent, so its real input must be positive. Exponent and logarithm rules follow from products and powers; they do not turn a sum into a sum of logarithms.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

In aˣ, a is the base and x exponent. logₐ b is the exponent giving b: aˣ = b ⇔ x = logₐ b, for a > 0, a ≠ 1 and b > 0. ln is logarithm with base e ≈ 2.71828; log₁₀ uses base 10. ⇔ means equivalent statements; a superscript is a power, not multiplication by that number.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. 2³ = 8, so log₂(8) = 3.
  2. Solve 2ˣ = 10 using x = ln(10)/ln(2) ≈ 3.322.
  3. Check by substituting the value back into the original equation.
02 · Recognise arithmetic and geometric change

An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference between successive terms. A geometric sequence has a constant ratio when that ratio is defined. Identify the starting term and whether the index counts terms or elapsed periods before selecting a formula.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A sequence is an ordered list; uₙ is its nth term and n a positive integer index. Arithmetic: uₙ = u₁ + (n − 1)d, with first term u₁ and common difference d. Geometric: uₙ = u₁rⁿ⁻¹ with common ratio r. Sₙ denotes sum of first n terms. A ratio is multiplicative change, different from a fixed additive difference.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For 2, 5, 8, … the nth term is 2 + 3(n − 1).
  2. For 200, 208, 216.32, … the nth term is 200 × 1.04ⁿ⁻¹.
  3. Adding 4 each time would be a different model from increasing by 4%.
03 · Solve a model and respect its context

Numerical solving can provide a value where an expression is zero or two expressions are equal. Check the original domain, inspect relevant intervals and substitute to verify. The practical interpretation may require an inequality or whole-number answer after solving the equality.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A model uses variables for contextual quantities and parameters for fixed settings. In y = a bˣ, a is value at x = 0 and b growth factor per unit x. Growth rate r gives b = 1 + r, where r is a fraction, not the numeric percent. Reject algebraic solutions outside contextual domains, such as negative counts.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For 1000 × 1.05ᵗ = 1500, t = ln(1.5)/ln(1.05) ≈ 8.31 years.
  2. If interest is credited only at yearly endpoints, the first whole year meeting the target is year 9.
  3. State the assumed compounding rule before applying the result.
