Statistics describes and draws conclusions from data. A variable records a characteristic, a population is the group of interest and a sample is a subset observed. A mean measures one kind of centre; a standard deviation describes spread. Neither captures every feature of a distribution.

Choose a method suited to the variable and model. Sample and population standard deviations answer different questions. A probability model requires assumptions, such as independent trials and constant success probability for a binomial count. A p-value describes compatibility with a specified null model; it is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

A school survey can show association between study time and results, but a fitted regression alone does not prove that one causes the other. Selection of participants, measurement quality and other variables matter. Predicting far beyond the observed data adds an extrapolation risk.

01 · Describe data before fitting a model

A data summary should describe centre, spread and shape, and consider unusual values. A mean uses every value; a median uses ordered position. A population standard deviation describes the whole listed population, while a sample estimate uses a different convention.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A population is the full group of interest; a sample is the subset observed. Mean x̄ = Σx/n uses observations x, count n and sum symbol Σ. Standard deviation measures spread around the mean; sample s and population σ use different denominators. A quartile splits ordered data; calculator interpolation conventions can differ and should be stated.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For 2, 3, 4, 5, 16 the mean is 6 and the median is 4.
  2. The large value pulls the mean upward.
  3. A single average hides the difference between a tight cluster and a widely spread set.
02 · Match the probability model to the experiment

A binomial model counts successes in a fixed number of independent trials with constant success probability. A normal model is continuous and uses a mean and standard deviation. A calculator distribution command does not establish whether the assumptions are appropriate.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

P(A) is probability of event A; A∩B means both events, A∪B at least one, and A′ complement. P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B), for P(B) > 0, is conditional probability. X names a random variable. Binomial X ~ B(n,p) assumes n independent trials, two outcomes and fixed success probability p; normal X ~ N(μ,σ²) uses mean μ and variance σ².

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. For X binomial with n = 5 and p = 0.2, P(X = 0) = 0.8⁵ = 0.32768.
  2. Therefore P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − 0.32768 = 0.67232.
  3. Using a cumulative probability requires translating the inequality carefully.
03 · Use regression without inventing causation

Regression models association between variables and can support prediction within an appropriate range. Residuals compare observations with model predictions. Correlation alone does not establish cause; a strong fit can still reflect confounding, selection or a limited data range.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Regression fits a relationship, such as predicted ŷ = a + bx, where a is intercept and b slope in y-units per x-unit. A residual is y − ŷ. Correlation coefficient r lies between −1 and 1 and describes linear association, not causation. Extrapolation predicts outside observed input values and can be unreliable.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. If a fitted cost rule is C = 4 + 2x, the intercept is a fixed model cost and the slope is 2 cost units per x unit.
  2. An observation C = 13 at x = 4 has residual 13 − 12 = 1.
  3. Predicting for an x far beyond the data is extrapolation.
04 · Interpret inference in the stated model

A p-value is a probability, under a null model, of data at least as incompatible in the chosen test direction. It is not the probability that the null is true. A frequentist confidence procedure is assessed by repeated-sampling coverage, not by assigning a probability to a fixed parameter after observing one interval.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A hypothesis is a statistical claim. H₀ is null hypothesis and H₁ alternative. A p-value is probability, under H₀ and its assumptions, of results at least as extreme as observed; it is not probability H₀ is true. Significance level α is a chosen decision threshold. A confidence interval is produced by a method with stated long-run coverage, not a probability assigned to a fixed parameter after observing the interval.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. A p-value of 0.03 is compared with a prespecified significance level.
  2. At level 0.05 it may lead to rejection under the test conditions; at level 0.01 it would not.
  3. State the assumptions, conclusion in context and limits rather than saying a hypothesis has been proved.
