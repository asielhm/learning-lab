Numbers II
Preparation and worked examples

A signed number tells you which side of a chosen zero you mean. Positive and negative do not mean good and bad: they can represent above and below sea level, or money added and money owed. On a number line, values increase towards the right. −8 is less than −3 even though its distance from zero is greater.

Adding a signed change moves from a starting value. Subtracting a change means adding its opposite. Multiplying by a negative number reverses sign, so two sign reversals give a positive result. Brackets matter in powers: (−3)² = 9, whereas −3² = −9 because the square applies before the leading minus sign.

A temperature of −2 °C rising by 5 °C becomes +3 °C. An account at −£4 receiving £10 becomes +£6. These are starting values plus signed changes; neither story asks you to ignore the minus sign or replace every negative number with its magnitude.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A number set is a collection of numbers. Here ℕ means whole numbers from 0 upwards; some textbooks start at 1, so check the convention. ℤ is all integers, including negative whole numbers. ℚ is rational numbers: numbers expressible as a/b with integers a,b and b ≠ 0. ⊂ means “is contained in”; ∈ means “belongs to”. A finite decimal such as 0.5 is rational because it equals 1/2.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A number line places numbers in increasing order from left to right. Positive means above zero; negative means below zero. Adding a positive number moves right; adding a negative moves left. Subtracting a number means adding its opposite, so 3 − (−2) = 3 + 2. A displacement is a signed change, not the total distance travelled.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

The base is the quantity being raised to a power. Brackets matter: (−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9, but −3² means −(3²) = −9. An even exponent gives an even count of negative factors; an odd exponent leaves a negative result for a negative base. A nonzero base to power 0 equals 1.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An integer calculation can contain positive and negative whole numbers. Deal with brackets and powers first, then multiplication/division from left to right, then addition/subtraction from left to right. Multiplying or dividing two values of the same sign gives positive; opposite signs give negative. This sign rule applies to products and quotients, not directly to addition.
