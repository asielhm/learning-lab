GeoGebra guide: construct your own perpendicular bisector
  1. Open GeoGebra Geometry. Use the Segment tool to create endpoints A and B.
  2. Choose Circle with Centre through Point. Make one circle centred at A through B, and another centred at B through A. Their radii are equal to AB.
  3. Use Intersect to mark the two circle intersections C and D.
  4. Draw the line through C and D. This is the perpendicular bisector of AB.
  5. Use the Midpoint tool and Angle tool to check the midpoint and 90° angle. Drag A or B with Move: explain why the relationship is preserved.
GeoGebra guide: construct an angle bisector
  1. Draw two rays with a common vertex O.
  2. Draw a circle centred at O. Mark where it crosses the two rays as P and Q.
  3. Draw two equal-radius circles: one centred at P through Q and one centred at Q through P.
  4. Choose their intersection S inside the angle and farther from O. Draw the ray OS.
  5. Measure the two new angles and move the original rays. Use the equal distances OP = OQ and PS = QS to explain the bisection.
