Geometry I
Preparation and worked examples

Geometry describes position, shape and size. A point marks a position, a line segment joins two points, and an angle describes a turn between directions. Shape names tell you properties, such as equal sides or parallel edges; a drawing that looks convincing is not a proof. Read the stated properties before calculating.

Perimeter counts distance around a boundary, while area counts how many square units cover a region. Splitting a polygon into triangles explains its interior-angle sum: (n − 2) × 180°. Dividing that total by n finds one interior angle only when the polygon is regular. For compound shapes, include every exposed boundary in the perimeter and avoid counting shared internal edges.

A rectangular garden 4 m by 3 m needs 14 m of fencing but has 12 m² of ground to cover. A 5 m by 2 m garden needs the same fence length and has a different area, 10 m². A builder ordering paving and fencing must distinguish these quantities and units.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A side is a straight boundary segment; a vertex is a corner. A triangle has three sides; a quadrilateral has four. Parallel lines stay the same distance apart; perpendicular lines meet at 90°. Equal sides have equal lengths. A right angle is 90°; acute is less than 90° and obtuse is between 90° and 180°.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Perimeter P is the distance around the boundary, measured in length units such as cm. Area A measures the surface covered, in square units such as cm². For a rectangle, A = b × h: b is base length and h perpendicular height. For a triangle, A = ½bh: half the area of the matching rectangle. Height must meet the base at a right angle; a sloping side is not usually the height.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A polygon is a closed shape made of straight sides. n is its number of sides. A diagonal joins non-neighbouring vertices. Splitting from one vertex gives n − 2 triangles, so the interior-angle sum is (n − 2) × 180°. A regular polygon has all sides and interior angles equal; each interior angle is that sum divided by n. ° means degrees.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A transversal is a line crossing two other lines. With parallel lines, corresponding angles occupy matching positions; alternate angles lie on opposite sides of the transversal and are equal; co-interior angles inside the parallels on the same side add to 180°. Angles on a straight line total 180°; angles around a point total 360°.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

To bisect means to divide into two equal parts. An angle bisector splits an angle equally. A perpendicular bisector crosses a segment at its midpoint at 90°. A compass draws arcs with a fixed radius, the distance from its point to its pencil; equal-radius arcs construct equal distances rather than estimate them by eye.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A compound shape combines simpler shapes. Split its area into rectangles or triangles and add their areas, or subtract a missing piece. Use the same length unit throughout. Only exposed outer edges count towards perimeter; an internal dividing line is not part of the outside boundary. Label missing lengths using aligned totals before calculating.

01

Triangles and quadrilaterals

Understand the ideas

  • Equilateral means all three sides are equal.
  • Every rectangle has four right angles; equal adjacent sides are not required.
  • A square has four right angles and four equal sides.
  • Its two symmetry lines pass through the midpoints of opposite sides.
  • Each vertex-to-opposite-midpoint line is a symmetry line.
  • A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
  • This uses the stated exclusive definition; conventions can differ between textbooks.
  • Equal sides in a triangle have equal opposite angles.
  • A rhombus has equal sides, but need not have right angles.
  • The equal sides in a kite meet at adjacent vertices.

04

Bisector constructions

Understand the ideas

  • Perpendicular specifies 90°; bisector specifies the midpoint.
  • Equal radii make the arc intersections equidistant from A and B.
  • The arcs need two intersections, one on each side of the segment.
  • Joining the intersections produces the perpendicular bisector.
  • Equal distance from the endpoints characterises the perpendicular bisector.
  • An angle bisector halves the original angle.
  • The arcs establish equal distances and the straightedge joins the required points.
  • Together with the first equal-radius arc, this gives congruent triangles.
  • The construction should be justified by equal distances, not a measured approximation.
  • Changing the radius breaks the equal-distance argument.

Understand the ideas

  • 10 × 8 − 4 × 3 = 68 cm².
  • 9 × 7 − 3 × 2 = 57 cm².
  • 12 × 8 − 5 × 4 = 76 cm².
  • Removing a rectangular corner replaces two boundary lengths with equal inward lengths; total = 2(width + height).
  • Add the three non-overlapping areas: 12 + 15 + 8 = 35.
  • 12 + 10 + 6 = 28 cm².
  • 26 − 3 = 23 cm².
  • Each perimeter is 10 cm, but the shared 2 cm edge is internal and counted twice: 20 − 4 = 16 cm.
  • Trace only the boundary, or subtract twice every shared edge.
