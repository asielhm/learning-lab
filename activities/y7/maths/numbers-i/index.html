Whole numbers help us count objects, arrange equal groups and describe repeated events. A factor divides a number exactly; a multiple is made by multiplying it by a whole number. A prime has exactly two positive factors, so 1 is neither prime nor composite. Start by making small lists before using faster methods.

Prime factorisation describes a number using prime building blocks. For the HCF, keep only factors shared by both numbers, with the smaller repeated count. For the LCM, include enough prime factors to build either number. Powers mean repeated multiplication, not multiplication by the exponent. In BIDMAS, multiplication and division have equal priority and run left to right; addition and subtraction do too.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

If 24 pens and 36 clips must be packed into identical kits with none left over, the greatest possible number of kits is HCF(24,36) = 12. Each kit has 2 pens and 3 clips. If lights flash every 24 s and 36 s, their next shared flash is after LCM(24,36) = 72 s. The same numbers answer different questions.

01 · Prime building blocks, HCF and LCM

Compare factor lists, prime factorisations and multiples of two whole numbers.

List divisors for HCF and multiplication-table entries for LCM. A prime number has exactly two positive factors; 1 is neither prime nor composite.

The tiles are the common factors. The largest is 12. 72 is the smallest positive multiple shared by both numbers; it may be beyond the six multiples listed.

HCF = 12 · LCM = 72

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An integer is a whole number, including negatives and zero. A factor divides a number exactly; a multiple is a number multiplied by an integer. A prime has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. HCF means highest common factor; LCM means lowest common multiple. Prime factorisation writes a whole number as a product of primes. The symbol × means multiply; a superscript counts repeated factors, so 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2.

24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3

Composite: more than two positive factors.

Factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24

Multiples: 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 144…

36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3

Composite: more than two positive factors.

Factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36

Multiples: 36, 72, 108, 144, 180, 216…

1234612

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. List factors and choose the largest common one: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.
  2. Prime factors: 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3; 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.
  3. LCM = (24 × 36) ÷ HCF = 864 ÷ 12 = 72.
02 · Build a decimal with place value

See tenths and hundredths as parts of one whole, rather than as separate whole numbers.

Read 1.37 as one whole and thirty-seven hundredths. It is not “one point thirty-seven” as a whole-number value.

Digits get ten times smaller in value as you move one place to the right. A zero can hold a place without changing the other digits.

1.37 = 1 + 3/10 + 7/100

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Place value tells you how much a digit is worth from its position. In 3.47, 3 means three ones, 4 means four tenths and 7 means seven hundredths. The decimal point separates whole units from parts. A tenth is 1/10 of a unit; a hundredth is 1/100. A zero can hold a place without adding value.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. 1 complete grids represent 1 whole(s).
  2. 3 full columns in the next grid represent 3/10.
  3. 7 additional cells represent 7/100.
03 · Squares, cubes and their roots

Connect powers to repeated multiplication and roots to the inverse operation.

The index tells you how many equal factors to multiply. It does not mean multiply the base by the index.

A square counts 4 rows of 4; a cube has 4 equal layers of that square. The cube drawing is a schematic solid, not individual unit cubes.

4² = 16 · 4³ = 64

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A power has a base and an exponent: in x³, x is the base and 3 is the exponent. A square multiplies a number by itself; a cube uses three equal factors. √ is the non-negative square root: √25 = 5 because 5² = 25. ∛ is cube root: ∛27 = 3. A square number can form a square array; a cube number can form a solid cube.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. 4² means 4 × 4 = 16.
  2. 4³ means 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
  3. √16 = 4; ∛64 = 4.
04 · Powers, then multiplication, then addition

Follow the order of operations; multiplication and division share priority, as do addition and subtraction.

Write one line per operation. Square the whole bracket before multiplying.

Read left to right between operations of equal priority. BIDMAS does not mean always divide before multiplying or always add before subtracting.

6 + 3 × 2² = 18

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An expression is a calculation without an equals sign. BIDMAS is the order: Brackets, Indices (powers and roots), Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction. Division and multiplication share priority and run left to right; so do addition and subtraction. A bracket groups a calculation; the equals sign means both sides have the same value, not merely “next step”.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Index first: 2² = 4.
  2. Multiply next: 3 × 4 = 12.
  3. Add last: 6 + 12 = 18.
