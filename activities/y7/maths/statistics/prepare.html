Statistics
Preparation and worked examples

Data are observations collected to answer a question. A variable is what is recorded, such as travel method or number of siblings. Discrete numerical data are counts that take separate values. Frequency f means how many times a category or value occurs, and total frequency N counts all observations. State the question and labels before drawing a chart.

A pictogram uses a key to say how many observations each full symbol represents. A bar chart compares frequencies using bar heights on a labelled scale. A pie chart shows shares of one whole: sector angle = (f/N) × 360°. Mean is total divided by N; median is the sorted middle; mode is the most frequent value; range is largest minus smallest. Here Q₁ and Q₃ are medians of the lower and upper halves, excluding the overall median when N is odd.

A class travel survey might count 6 bus users, 8 walkers, 4 car users and 2 cyclists: N = 20. A pictogram key of 2 needs 3 bus symbols. Walkers occupy 8/20 = 40% of a pie chart, so their sector is 144°. The data describe this class, not automatically every student in a city. A small sample or an unclear question can limit a conclusion.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Data are recorded observations. A category groups observations of the same kind. Frequency is how many observations belong to a category. A pictogram uses repeated pictures; its key states the count per whole picture. If one picture means 4 pupils, half a picture means 2, not half a pupil. Frequency = number of pictures × key value.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A bar chart compares categories. One axis lists categories and the other has a numbered frequency scale. A bar height records the count; all bars use the same scale and equal width, with gaps between categories. Discrete data take separate possible values, such as number of siblings; a frequency table lists how often each value occurs.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A pie chart shows parts of a whole in a 360° circle. N is the total frequency and f the frequency of one category. Sector angle = (f/N) × 360°; percentage = (f/N) × 100%. A sector is the wedge for one category; all sector angles must total 360°. The denominator is the total number of observations, not the number of category names.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Order data from smallest to largest first. Mean = sum of values/count; median is the middle value (or mean of the two central values); mode is every value tied for highest frequency, with no mode here if all occur once; range = largest − smallest. Q1 and Q3 are medians of lower and upper halves; this lesson excludes the overall median when splitting an odd-sized list. For a frequency table, N = Σf and mean = Σ(fx)/N: x is a data value, f its frequency and Σ means add over rows.
