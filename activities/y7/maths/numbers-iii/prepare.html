Numbers III
Preparation and worked examples

A fraction compares a part with a specified whole, and its denominator tells you how that whole is divided into equal parts. A decimal is another way to express a number; a percentage means per hundred. A ratio compares quantities and needs its order and total parts interpreted carefully.

Equivalent fractions multiply numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor. For addition, use equal-sized parts through a common denominator; multiplication has a different rule. A percentage increase or discount is measured from a stated original amount, not from the new amount. A map scale compares lengths in the same units, not areas by the same scale factor.

Sharing £20 in the ratio 1:3 means four total parts worth £5 each, giving £5 and £15. A 25% discount on £40 removes £10 and leaves £30. On a 1:1000 map, 3 cm represents 3000 cm = 30 m; using centimetres on one side and metres on the other without conversion changes the meaning.

  1. Read and explain. Learn the vocabulary and say why the process works.
  2. Work through an example. Identify the known information, rule and result.
  3. Investigate, then apply. Return to the practical, game or revision simulator.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A ratio compares quantities in an order, such as red:blue = 2:3. The colon means “to”; it is not an instruction to add. There are 2 + 3 = 5 equal shares altogether. For a total T, one share is T/5; red is 2T/5 and blue 3T/5. A three-part ratio works the same way after adding all three share counts.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A map scale 1:n means 1 map length unit represents n of the same units in reality; n is a scale factor, not a distance. Real distance = map distance × n. Convert units after multiplying: 100 cm = 1 m and 1000 m = 1 km. A scale bar labels real distances and must be read in its stated units.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

In a fraction a/b, a is the numerator (how many parts) and b the denominator (how many equal parts form one whole), with b ≠ 0. Equivalent fractions have the same value. A decimal uses tenths, hundredths and so on. Percent means “per hundred”: p% = p/100. Thus 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%, three ways of writing the same share.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Rational numbers can be written as fractions of integers. To add fractions, first express them in a common denominator; to multiply, multiply numerators and denominators. The reciprocal of nonzero a/b is b/a. Dividing by a nonzero fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal. Cancel only common factors in products, not individual parts of a sum.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Original amount is the starting value; percentage change is measured relative to that value. With p as the numerical percent, an increase multiplies by 1 + p/100 and a discount by 1 − p/100. For 20%, p = 20 and p/100 = 0.20. A multiplier is the factor applied to the whole amount. A later 20% decrease does not undo a 20% increase because its starting amount differs.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

ℚ⁺ means positive rational numbers. BIDMAS applies to fractions and decimals in the same order as whole numbers. A fraction bar groups the entire numerator and denominator; calculate those groups before dividing. An index is an exponent, so (1/2)² = 1/4. Keep exact fractions during calculation to avoid rounding changes.
