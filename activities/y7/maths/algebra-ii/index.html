Build the idea, one step at a time.

A variable is a symbol for a number that can change or is not yet known. An expression describes a quantity, while an equation states that two expressions are equal. A term is a part separated by addition or subtraction, a coefficient multiplies a variable part, and a constant term has no variable. Start by translating a story before trying to solve anything.

Collect only terms with the same letters and powers. When multiplying terms, multiply numerical coefficients and count repeated factors of each letter: 2a × 3a = 6a², but 2a × 3b = 6ab. Expand a bracket by multiplying every term inside, including its sign. To solve an equation, make equivalent equations: apply the same valid operation to both sides. N denotes the natural numbers used in the course, while Z includes positive and negative integers and zero. An integer solution can be negative even when an earlier exercise used only positive values.

Connect it to everyday life and industry

If three identical school packs each contain x pens and two loose pens, the expression is 3(x + 2). If there are 21 pens in total, the equation is 3(x + 2) = 21. Divide both sides by 3 to get x + 2 = 7, then subtract 2 from both sides: x = 5. Substitution checks 3(5 + 2) = 21.

01 · Collect terms by letter and power

Add coefficients of identical variable parts; keep unlike terms separate.

Underline the variable part before adding coefficients.

A term is one part joined to other terms by addition or subtraction. Its coefficient counts copies of its variable part. x, x² and x³ are different variable parts. A negative coefficient means a subtraction, not a negative physical length.

x coefficient: 3 + 2 = 5

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An expression names a value without asserting an equality. A term includes its sign; a coefficient is its numerical multiplier. An exponent tells how many equal variable factors are multiplied: x² = x × x and x³ = x × x × x. Like terms have the same letters to the same powers. Adding coefficients changes the number of equal pieces, not their powers: 2x² + 3x² = 5x².

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. The terms 3x and 2x both have variable part x.
  2. Add their coefficients: 3 + (2) = 5.
  3. Keep 4y separate because its variable part differs.
02 · Add and multiply algebraic terms

Start with numbers, introduce variables and compare adding like terms with multiplying factors.

Start with numbers: 2 × 3 = 6. The numbers 2 and 3 are factors, the quantities being multiplied. Their answer, 6, is called the product. Multiplication can represent equal groups or a rectangular array: two rows of three objects contain six objects.

Now introduce a variable. A variable is a letter standing for a number, such as a. We write 2a as a short way to write 2 × a. The numerical multiplier 2 is the coefficient. In 2a × 3, rearrange the factors as 2 × 3 × a = 6a. Only one factor of a is present, so there is no square.

What if the letter appears twice? a × a means multiply the value of a by itself. We shorten this to a², read “a squared”. The small raised 2 is an exponent: it counts TWO equal factors, not two copies being added. Typed a^2 means the same as a²; the caret ^ introduces the exponent.

Check with a number: let a = 4. Then a × a = 4 × 4 = 16, so a² = 16. But 2a = 2 × 4 = 8. Thus a² and 2a are different expressions. For a positive length a, a square with side length a has area a × a = a², which explains the name “squared”.

Now multiply two variable terms: 2a × 3a = (2 × a) × (3 × a). Multiplication lets us reorder and group the factors: (2 × 3) × (a × a) = 6a². The 6 comes from 2 × 3; the square comes from a × a. With a = 4, the original gives 8 × 12 = 96 and the simplified product gives 6 × 16 = 96.

For different letters, 2a × 3b = (2 × 3) × (a × b) = 6ab. ab means a × b. We cannot replace ab by a² unless we know b equals a; different letters may stand for different numbers. Conventionally, put the numerical coefficient first and letters in alphabetical order.

Adding variables means counting like quantities. a + a = 2a because there are two copies of a. In 2a + 3a, combine two copies and three copies to get five: (2 + 3)a = 5a. The letter part stays a. Likewise, 2a² + 3a² = 5a² because the identical pieces are a². But a and a² are different variable parts, so 2a² + 3a cannot be collected into one term.

Do not mix addition and multiplication: 2a + 3a = 5a counts five copies of a, while 2a × 3a = 6a² contains two factors of a. In a² × a, write a × a × a to see why the result is a³. Count repeated factors of each letter separately.

Start with 2 × 3. Then attach one a, two a factors, or one a and one b. a² means a × a, not 2a. The coefficient of a is 1 when no number is shown.

A coefficient is the numerical multiplier of a variable part. Separate the numerical and letter factors, multiply the numbers and keep every variable factor. a × a = a², while a × b = ab. Here × is multiplication; the letter x is a variable. Adding like terms is a different operation: 2a + 3a = 5a, but 2a × 3a = 6a².

Simplified product: 6a

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A factor is multiplied by another factor; the product is the result. In 2a, 2 is the coefficient and a a variable number; 2a = 2 × a. A constant has no variable. In a², a is the base and 2 the exponent: a × a. a^2 is its typed form. The exponent does not multiply the value by 2. ab = a × b, and x in a term is a letter while × is the multiplication sign. Multiplying reorders factors; adding like terms adds coefficients instead. a + a = 2a counts two copies; a × a = a² multiplies two factors. The sum 2a + 3b stays as two unlike terms.

Numbers times numbers and variables times variables.

For multiplication: multiply the numerical coefficients, then count each letter’s factors. Keep every factor: 2a × 3a = 6a², while 2a × 3b = 6ab.

For addition: collect LIKE terms; keep their variable part.

Add the coefficients only when letters AND powers match: 2a + 3a = 5a. But 2a + 3b stays as two terms, and a + a = 2a rather than a².

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Identify each factor: 2a × 3. A factor is a quantity being multiplied.
  2. Multiply the numerical coefficients: 2 × 3 = 6.
  3. Keep and count the letters: a. Reordering factors does not change their product.

03 · Expand every term in the bracket

Use the distributive law with positive and negative constants.

Draw one multiplication arrow from the outside factor to each term.

The outside factor multiplies every term inside the brackets, including each sign. Expansion changes how an expression is written while preserving its value for every x. Expanding is not the same task as solving an equation.

x coefficient 6 · constant -6

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Brackets group terms. To expand a(b + c), multiply a into both b and c, giving ab + ac. Here a,b,c stand for quantities and adjacent letters mean multiplication. A negative multiplier changes every sign: −2(x − 3) = −2x + 6. Expand first, then collect like terms; keep x² separate from x.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Multiply the variable term: 3 × 2x = 6x.
  2. Multiply the constant: 3 × (-2) = -6.
  3. Write both results together, keeping the sign of -6.
04 · Follow equivalent equations, one step at a time

Preserve equality by doing the same valid operation to both sides.

Do not move a term magically. Name the operation done to both sides.

An equation states that two expressions have equal value. A solution is an x value making the original equality true. Expand brackets, collect x terms on one side, remove constants and divide by the remaining coefficient when it is nonzero. The workbench below lets you choose your own operations.

x = 7

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An equation says two expressions have equal value; a solution is a value that makes that statement true. Equivalent equations have the same solutions. To keep balance, add or subtract the same quantity on both sides, or divide both sides by the same nonzero number. In ax + b = cx + d, a and c are coefficients of x, b and d constants. Substitution means replacing every x by your proposed value and comparing both sides.

Worked example · follow the reasoning

  1. Add -5 to BOTH sides to remove the constant from the left. x = 7
