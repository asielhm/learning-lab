Algebra I
Preparation and worked examples

A variable is a symbol for a quantity that can change or is not yet known. An expression describes a value; an equation states that two values are equal. A table, a formula and a graph can describe the same relationship. Decide first what each variable measures, including its units.

Coordinates are ordered pairs (x,y): horizontal position first, then vertical position. Direct proportion has a constant ratio y/x and can be written y = kx; its graph goes through the origin. Inverse proportion has a constant product xy and can be written y = k/x for nonzero x. An increasing relationship is not automatically direct proportion, especially if it includes a fixed starting amount.

At £4 per notebook, three notebooks cost £12: the point (3,12) belongs to y = 4x. A £4 delivery fee would change the total rule and break direct proportion. For a fixed 12 m² rectangle, width 3 m needs length 4 m and width 6 m needs length 2 m: the product, not the ratio, stays constant.

  2. Work through an example. Identify the known information, rule and result.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A variable is a quantity that can take different values; a letter stands for its value. A constant stays fixed in the model. A formula links quantities: if a notebook costs 4 units, y = 4x means total cost y equals 4 times notebook count x. The coefficient 4 multiplies x; writing 4x leaves out the multiplication sign. An expression such as 4x + 3 describes a value; an equation states two expressions are equal.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

An axis is a numbered reference line; axes is the plural. The x-axis is horizontal, across the page; the y-axis is vertical, up and down. Their crossing is the origin (0,0). An ordered pair (x,y) gives horizontal position first, then vertical position. (2,5) means 2 right and 5 up from the origin; it is different from (5,2). A scale tells you what each grid interval represents. P is simply a name used to label the plotted point.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Direct proportion means multiplying one quantity by a factor multiplies the other by the same factor. In y = kx, x is input, y output and k the constant of proportionality: k = y/x for nonzero x. For 4-unit notebooks, k = 4 cost units per notebook. The graph is a straight line through the origin because zero notebooks cost zero in this model; an extra fixed delivery charge breaks direct proportion.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

Inverse proportion means that the product stays fixed: xy = k, or y = k/x for x ≠ 0. For a 24 worker-hour job, x is number of equally productive workers and y hours per worker; k = 24 worker-hours. Doubling workers halves the time if they can share the work without delays. The positive-domain graph is a curve, not a straight line. Inverse does not mean “any relationship that decreases”.

Understand the ideas

  • Inverse proportion means xy = constant.
  • If y = k/x, then xy = k.
  • The constant product must be preserved.
  • Use the reciprocal scale factor.
  • Equal increases in x do not produce equal decreases in y.
  • A decreasing relationship alone does not prove inverse proportion; xy must be constant.
  • Fixed work and equal effective productivity are essential.
  • k = xy = 24.
  • xy = 36, so x = 36 ÷ 9 = 4.
  • 2 × 18 = 36; 36 ÷ 6 = 6.

Mystery Machine Mission · concepts and examples

A machine that scales

The rule is y = 3x. Multiplying by 3 and passing through is direct proportion: every output is three times its input.

A machine with a fixed addition

The rule is y = x + 4. The difference y − x stays 4. This is not direct proportion, because zero input gives output 4.

Order matters

The rule is y = 2x + 3. Double first, then add 3. Adding 3 first and doubling gives 2x + 6, a different rule.

A bracket in disguise

y = 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6. Add 2 then multiply by 3, or multiply by 3 then add 6: both produce the same output for every input.

Unpack a half-sized output

y = (x − 4)/2 = x/2 − 2. Both operation orders shown by these equivalent forms work. Halving an odd input can produce a non-integer output.

Challenge: the output moves backwards

y = −2x + 6. A negative multiplier reverses the direction of change. Multiplying by −2 is different from subtracting 2.

Proportion Paintworks · concepts and examples

Sunrise mural

3 blue + 6 gold = 9 units. Gold = 2 × blue, so their ratio stays 1 : 2. Doubling the batch doubles both amounts.

Lagoon delivery

8 : 12 simplifies to 2 : 3. Both colours were scaled by the same factor, 4. Equal additions to both colours would not generally preserve the recipe.

Ocean batch

9 blue and 6 gold preserve the ratio 3 : 2. The gold-versus-blue graph is a straight line through the origin: gold = (2/3) × blue.

Robots against the clock

3 robots × 2 panels/min × 2 min = 12 panels. For a fixed job and equal independent robots, more robots means less time: nT = 6 robot-minutes.

The long wall

4 robots × 1 panel/min × 5 min = 20 panels. Doubling the crew would halve the completion time, not double it. This ideal model assumes equal rates and no interference.

Festival finale · two models

Mix 8 blue with 4 gold. Then 4 robots × 2 panels/min × 3 min cover 24 panels. The recipe uses a constant ratio; crew and time use a constant product. Neither rule can replace the other.

Variable Spotter · concepts and examples

Buying water bottles

Number purchased, total price and total volume can vary. A unit price declared fixed is a constant in this situation. You may name either selected variable x, provided you state its meaning.

A bicycle journey

Time, distance and speed can vary. The fixed wheel count does not vary within this stated situation. Constant speed would be an extra assumption, not something guaranteed by the word bicycle.

Downloading a file

Time, amount received and transfer rate can vary. A fixed unit conversion is not a changing quantity. The amount received need not increase at a constant rate.

Filling a swimming pool

Time, water volume and depth can change, while the stated pool length remains fixed. Their relationships depend on geometry and how water is supplied.

Studying for a test

All three selected quantity types can vary. More study time does not guarantee a particular score or direct proportion; other factors and the assessment matter.

What is a variable?

A variable is a quantity that can change or take different values. A letter is a name for it; a constant is fixed in the stated situation.

Exit check · define x and y

x can mean number of bottles and y total price, or the labels can be reversed if clearly defined. Letters do not have permanent meanings across every problem.

Find and repair the impostor

The instruction was reversed. (4, 2) means 4 across and 2 up; moving 2 across and 4 up plots (2, 4).

Find ALL pairs with the same x-coordinate

A and C both have x = 2; B and D both have x = 5. Points with the same x lie on a vertical line, even when their y values differ.

Find ALL pairs with the same y-coordinate

A and D both have y = 5; B and C both have y = 2. Points with the same y lie on a horizontal line.

Which points are furthest to the right?

B and D share the largest x-coordinate, 5. A point’s y-coordinate does not determine how far right it is.

Which points are highest?

A and D share the largest y-coordinate, 5. Two points can be equally high while having different x-coordinates.
