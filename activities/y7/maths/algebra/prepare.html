Algebra
Preparation and worked examples

An unopened box can stand for an unknown but fixed number of objects. Let x be the number of pens in a pen box and y the number of clips in a clip box. x + 3 means one x-sized quantity plus three individual pens. It does not say that x itself is three.

A coefficient counts copies of a variable part: 3x means x + x + x. Like terms have exactly the same variable part, including powers. x² means x × x and is different from both x and x³. Identical bags represent brackets: three bags of x + 2 contain three x quantities and six loose items. The outside multiplier reaches every term inside.

If a school packs two identical bags, each with x pens and 3 loose pens, the total counts can be written 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 when counting all items. If a cost p belongs to each pen, distinguish the cost expression from the object count: variables must keep their stated meaning.

  1. Read and explain. Learn the vocabulary and say why the process works.
  2. Work through an example. Identify the known information, rule and result.
  3. Investigate, then apply. Return to the practical, game or revision simulator.

Read the symbols and units

Use this reference whenever a letter, subscript or unit in an example is new to you. A subscript is a smaller label, such as ₁ for the first state; an exponent is a power, such as ² for a square.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A letter stands for the unknown number inside one unopened box. x counts pens per pen box; y counts clips per clip box. x + 3 means one pen-box quantity plus three loose pens. x + y combines two unknown quantities; it does not mean two boxes of the same kind. A coefficient counts repeated equal quantities: 3x = x + x + x. Units and the story decide what a letter represents.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

A term is one part separated by + or − at the top level of an expression; its sign belongs to it. In −3x², −3 is the coefficient, x is the variable and 2 is the exponent (power). Like terms have identical letters and powers: 2x² + 5x² = 7x², but x² and x³ cannot be combined. x² means x × x; x³ means x × x × x. A constant term has no variable.

Words, symbols and units · unpack the idea

To expand means to remove brackets by multiplication. In a(b + c) = ab + ac, a multiplies every term inside; ab means a × b. For 3(x + 2), each of three bags holds x items plus 2 extras, giving 3x + 6. A minus sign is part of its term: 2(x − 3) = 2x − 6. Distributive means multiplication is distributed across addition or subtraction.

  • The unknown box count and the three known loose pens are added.
  • x + x = 2x. Two copies of x are not the same as x multiplied by x.
  • The two unknown counts are added. Different letters need not have equal values.

  • Subtract four from the starting number x.
  • The ticket price is counted three times, and the one booking fee is added once.
  • Six cards per packet multiplied by b packets gives 6b.
  • There are two copies of everything in one bag, so the whole sum x + 3 is multiplied by two.
  • Twice y is 2y; three more than that is 2y + 3.

Algebraic vocabulary

Understand the ideas

  • An expression describes a quantity without stating equality. An equation contains an equals sign.
  • The coefficient multiplies the variable: 5x means x + x + x + x + x.
  • The terms are a, +2b and −3c²; their signs belong to the terms.
  • A constant term has no variable. The 4 is a coefficient, not a separate term.
  • The exponent tells us how many copies of the base are multiplied.
  • Area is side × side = x × x = x². The perimeter would be 4x.
  • Volume is length × width × height = y × y × y = y³.
  • Adding two copies of x is always equivalent to 2x; the other statements hold only for particular values.
  • The formula links the variables by the rule n = x + 3.
  • The signed numerical factor is −4 and the variable part is z³.

Understand the ideas

  • The last y has coefficient 1, so 7 − 2 + 1 = 6.
  • Add the signed coefficients: 4 + 1 − 3 = 2.
  • Like terms have exactly the same variable part; coefficients may differ.
  • The x terms give 3x and the y terms give 2y.
  • The constants 5 and 3 combine, while 2x stays separate.
  • Different variables cannot generally be combined as copies of x.
  • The signed coefficients add to zero: 6 − 2 − 4 = 0.
  • There are four a terms and seven units. Different letters follow the same rule.
  • Collect only matching variable parts: 5x − 3x = 2x and 2y + y = 3y.

04

Squares cubes and like terms

Understand the ideas

  • Adding square terms changes their coefficient, not their exponent.
  • The first cube term has coefficient 1, so 1 + 4 = 5.
  • The same letter and exponent are required.
  • Square terms give 3x²; linear terms give 4x. They remain different groups.
  • Repeated addition produces a coefficient. A cube is repeated multiplication.
  • Only the cubic terms combine; the linear z term is different.
  • Each different variable and power has its own group. This is the classroom collecting-terms model.
  • The y² terms cancel, while the x² coefficients add to three.
  • For addition, both variable and exponent must match. Rules for multiplying powers do not apply to this sum.
  • Collect each group separately: cubic x terms, square y terms and the linear z term.

Expanding brackets

Understand the ideas

  • Two bags give two copies of x and two copies of three.
  • Multiply both 2x and y by two, matching the classroom boxes example.
  • There are five copies of a and five copies of three.
  • The factor 10 applies to both terms: 10 × 2c and 10 × 3.
  • Multiply the subtraction term too: 3 × (−2) = −6.
  • Every bag contributes x and 2y, so three bags contribute 3x and 6y.
  • An unwritten factor of −1 changes the sign of each term: −16 + b.
  • 4d × d = 4d², and 4d × (−4) = −16d.
  • Multiply every pair: e² + 3e − 9e − 27, then collect the linear terms.
  • The whole bag costs 3p + k + g, and two copies cost 2(3p + k + g).

06

Substitution and connections

Understand the ideas

  • Replace x by 20, then add three: 20 + 3 = 23.
  • Start with 20 pens and subtract the four lent out.
  • 2 × 5 + 3 = 13. The coefficient multiplies only its own term.
  • Two copies of four give eight; four multiplied by four gives sixteen.
  • Square x first: 3 × (2 × 2) = 12. The exponent does not apply to the coefficient.
  • 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
  • The example shows why different variables cannot generally be collected as copies of x.
  • Both forms count two copies of the entire group. Checking one value can expose a mistake, while the distributive law explains the identity.
  • Inside the bracket, 3 + 3 = 6; doubling it gives 12. The expanded form 2x + 6 also gives 12.
  • Like terms share the complete variable part. The values of the coefficients can differ.
